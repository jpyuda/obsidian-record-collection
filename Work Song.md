# Work Song

## Composition Info
- Composer: Nat Adderley (1960)
- Written for his album *Work Song* (Riverside)
- Lyrics added by Oscar Brown Jr. (1960) - chain gang/prison work song theme
- Became a soul jazz/funk standard rather than interpretive standard
- Simple, repetitive melody perfect for groove-based improvisation

## Versions in Collection

### Cannonball Adderley - The Japanese Concerts/Nippon Soul (1963)
- Recording: Tokyo, July 14, 1963
- Format: LP
- Personnel: Cannonball (alto), Nat Adderley (cornet), Joe Zawinul (piano), Sam Jones (bass), Louis Hayes (drums), Yusef Lateef (flute/oboe/tenor)
- Notes: Classic quintet + Lateef, live energy
- Folder: Saxophone

### Monty Alexander - Montreux Alexander
- Recording: Montreux Jazz Festival
- Format: LP
- Notes: Caribbean jazz piano approach to the groove
- Folder: Piano

## Listening Focus
- **The groove**: How different artists handle the repetitive, hypnotic quality
- **Nat vs. Cannonball**: Brother cornetist's original vs. alto saxophonist's takeover
- **Vocal vs. instrumental**: Does Oscar Brown Jr.'s chain gang framing change the meaning?
- **Soul jazz vehicle**: How does this work on organ, in Latin context?

## Collection Strategy
- This is an **Adderley brothers story** first
- 5-7 versions total (fewer than Poinciana - less interpretive range)
- Focus: Origin → peak performances → interesting genre variations
- Not chasing every version - the groove is consistent, looking for distinctive approaches

## Notes
- Not in Gioia's *Jazz Standards* - more of a soul jazz anthem than interpretive standard
- Beware Hozier's 2014 folk-rock song with same title (different composition)
- The simplicity/repetition is the point - it's a vamp for soloists, not a complex melody to reinterpret
